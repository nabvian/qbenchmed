Does quantum optimisation help on a real biomedical decision problem?

Given biomarkers you could acquire and the clinical outcomes a rule engine can reach, which biomarkers cover the most outcomes? Classical solvers and QAOA get the identical QUBO, so any difference between them comes from the optimiser, not the problem. Everything below runs the real qbm package, live in your browser.

Run it yourself

Every solver, one QUBO

Generate an instance, build one QUBO, and let every method minimise that object. Exhaustive enumeration of the same QUBO supplies the certified optimum. Greedy is labelled native: it works on the coverage structure and is scored on the QUBO axis by completing the auxiliary variables optimally.

QAOA depth

The default settings take well under a minute; 12 inputs with every depth can take a few minutes, because QAOA is simulated in WebAssembly. The state-vector simulator stops at 12 inputs here and at 16 in the published runs. Only regimes whose outcomes each depend on at most two biomarkers are offered: they admit the compact pairwise QUBO (at most 15 qubits). The other five regimes need the slack encoding at 23 to 79 qubits, beyond any state-vector simulator.

The real instance · QBMED-HEME-001

Where greedy stalls

66 biomarkers and 88 outcomes from an audit export of a working pathology engine's trigger matrix (no patient data). 86 of its 116 rule arms fire only when several biomarkers are present together, so coverage is not submodular. Greedy stops at 94.32% however much budget it gets, while the certified branch-and-bound reaches the 96.59% ceiling.

Published result · why QAOA does not do better

The penalty swamps the signal

The budget constraint is encoded as a penalty. Those terms run 85 to 374 times larger than the coverage signal, leaving under 1% of the energy spectrum holding anything useful. A constrained ansatz with no penalty (Dicke start, XY ring mixer) beats a random valid panel on every instance; the penalty version almost always does worse than guessing.

Status, honestly

  • No quantum advantage is claimed. Everything quantum here is noiseless state-vector simulation; nothing has run on hardware.
  • Nothing extrapolates beyond 16 inputs, and at the sizes that can be simulated greedy still often finds the optimum.
  • A covering panel solves a coverage objective over a rule graph. It does not mean any biomarker is clinically unnecessary. Q-BenchMed is not a clinical validator or a diagnostic device.
